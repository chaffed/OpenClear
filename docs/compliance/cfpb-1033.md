# CFPB Section 1033

*Current as of 2026-10-22. Checked against its sources at least once a year (control check `regulatory_currency`).*

**In short: OpenClear isn't a data provider, and positive pay isn't covered.** The CFPB's personal
financial data rights rule (12 CFR Part 1033) covers *consumers*, meaning natural persons
(§ 1033.131), and *Regulation E accounts*: accounts held primarily for personal, family or household
purposes (§ 1033.111(b)). Positive pay serves business accounts. A data provider is the institution
that offers the consumer the account (§ 1033.111(c)); OpenClear is the bank's vendor and offers
consumers nothing. If a bank puts a consumer account on positive pay, any duty under the rule is the
bank's, through its own interfaces (CUEC B-RA-3-02). Banks at or under the SBA's size standard are
outside the rule's main obligations anyway (§ 1033.111(d)). This isn't legal advice
([Compliance](README.md)).

**Status.** The CFPB is reconsidering the rule (advance notice of 22 August 2025). On 29 October
2025 a federal court (*Forcht Bank, N.A. v. CFPB*, E.D. Ky.) stayed its compliance dates until the
reconsideration ends. As of 22 October 2026 no new or interim rule has been published; this page is
revisited when one is.
