Users & Security Groups

OpenClear uses named, fully custom security groups instead of a fixed list of roles — each group is just a chosen set of permissions, so you can shape access exactly to how your organization is structured. Every new tenant is seeded with five starting groups: Admin (everything except a few deliberately opt-in permissions), Preparer, Approver, Viewer, and Bookkeeper (for outside staff who service one or more customers).

Managing security groups

Open Security Groups to create a new group, or edit an existing one's permission set. Changes take effect immediately for anyone in that group.

Managing users

{{ docs.screenshot("users", "Users list") }}
  • Create or edit a user — from Users, assign them a security group and (if applicable) a specific customer scope.
  • Let a customer manage its own people — on the customer's page, under Self-service. Its administrators (anyone you give a role with customer_user:manage) can then invite people, change their roles and accounts, and remove them, within that customer only. They can give only the roles you mark Offered to customers on the security group, and can't edit roles. They can't reset the password of, unlock or sign out anyone who also belongs elsewhere (your own staff included), or remove their last administrator. Everything they do is in your action log, and on the customer's page under Its people, sign-on and API client activity. Turning self-service off stops it at once.
  • Review access each quarter — Settings → Access reviews → Start a review. It lists every membership (your staff, your customers' people and your API clients) with its role, scope, accounts and last sign-in. Confirm each, say what you changed, or remove access (deactivated at once). When every one is decided the review is complete and recorded in the action log; the Controls page checks one is done each quarter. A customer that manages its own people reviews them the same way.
  • Approve new customer users — Settings → New customer users (off by default), or each customer's own setting on its page. When on, someone a customer's side adds (an invitation from its administrator, its own sign-on or its SCIM directory) can't sign in until you approve them under New customer users, which appears in the menu when anyone is waiting. Rejecting needs a reason and removes their access to that customer; the customer's administrators are told either way. Neither their sign-on, their directory nor their administrators can let them in. People you add yourself never wait.
  • Let a customer make its own API clients — the third switch under Self-service. Its administrators with customer_api_client:manage can then give their own systems credentials: a role you offer, all or some of their accounts, read only if they like, a secret or a key pair. Never acting for users or linked to a sign-on connection. Their clients are on your API clients page marked theirs, where you can revoke them; turning the switch off stops them making or changing clients, while those already made keep working until revoked.
  • Limit a customer's person to some accounts — from Edit, choose Choose accounts. They then see, change and are told about only those accounts' checks, ACH items and exceptions, and their bulk uploads reach only those accounts. It takes effect on their next request. Moving them to another customer gives them all of that customer's accounts until you limit them again.
  • Bulk import users — upload a file of multiple users at once.
  • Deactivate / reactivate / unlock — deactivate a user who's left, reactivate one who's returning, or unlock an account after too many failed login attempts.
  • Export — download the current user list as CSV or JSON.
  • Multi-customer access — grant an existing person (found by email) additional memberships across other customers, or even other bank tenants — useful for a bookkeeper who services several clients, or a bank employee who needs visibility into more than one customer.
