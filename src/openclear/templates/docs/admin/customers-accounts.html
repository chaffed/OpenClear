Customers & Accounts

A customer is one of your bank's own business clients — the layer between your organization (the tenant) and the individual bank accounts you're protecting. Customer records hold contact information and identifying details, and every account, issued item, exception, and setting ultimately belongs to one.

Decision cut-off

Items are decided by the cut-off: a time of day for each kind of item (checks, ACH), in the bank's time zone, set under Settings → Decision cut-off. Each item is due at the first cut-off on a business day that leaves at least the minimum review time; weekends, the Federal Reserve's holidays and the days you add as closed are skipped. Until the cut-off a decision can be changed or taken back; at the cut-off it becomes final and goes to your core through the decision feed, webhooks and decision files, never before.

A customer can have its own cut-off, agreed with you: set it on the customer's page, under To decide; blank uses the bank's. The Customers list shows each customer's cut-off and marks the agreed ones. Changing either is a protected setting when sensitive settings need a second admin, and affects only items that arrive afterwards. Every bank has a cut-off: until it sets its own, the installation's default applies (11:00 New York time unless your installation changed it), so even a bank deciding in real time has decisions that lock at a set time each business day.

How to

  • Add or edit a customer — from Customers, create a new client or update an existing one's details.
  • A customer's detail page links out to that customer's own ML settings, SSO configuration, unauthorized-debit statements, getting-started wizard, and data export — most per-customer administration starts there.

Reverse positive pay

For a customer who doesn't send an issued-check file, put the account on reverse positive pay: every check presented on it becomes an item for the customer to review (To review), the customer returns the ones they don't recognise by the cut-off, and the rest are paid. A check with a real problem (a stop, a duplicate, a void) still shows as that problem and follows the customer's default decision, so it's never paid by silence.

  1. Agree it with the customer in writing first, including that unreturned checks are paid (bank CUEC B-CA-3-02). The control check Reverse positive pay agreed lists the accounts on it, and those put on it in the last 30 days, for you to match against your agreements.
  2. On the customer's page, in Accounts, choose the account's Checks setting, then Reverse positive pay. Only your administrators can. It's a protected change: when your bank asks for a second administrator, it waits for one. Putting the account back on positive pay takes effect at once.
  3. Integrations can set it too: check_review_mode in PUT /api/v1/accounts/{account_number} (it answers 202 while it waits for approval).

Checks only for now. To review every ACH debit on an account, give it no ACH authorizations.

When a customer leaves or stops positive pay

OpenClear isn't the bank's system of record (the core is), so a customer's data is deleted 90 days after its last service day. On the customer's page, under Offboarding:

  1. Choose the last service day and End positive pay. A second administrator approves it under Changes. The customer's people are emailed and see the date on every page: they should take a data export before then.
  2. After that day, nobody at the customer can sign in, files that name its accounts are refused row by row (so the core's file shows it still sends them), and tellers are told its checks aren't on positive pay. Open exceptions take the account's default at their deadline. Stop the core sending its items too.
  3. 90 days later its data is deleted: the customer, its accounts, items, exceptions, decisions, images and uploads, and its people unless they belong elsewhere or are named in the bank's records. The bank's action log keeps what was done. The signed certificate is under Customers → Deleted customers.

Keep positive pay stops it before the last day. Restoring a customer whose service has ended, and taking its data off legal hold, need a second administrator; while it's on hold, nothing is deleted.
