Your People & Sign-on

If your bank lets your organization manage itself, its administrators see People, Sign-on and API clients in Settings, for what the bank allows.

People

  • Invite someone by email, choosing their role (one your bank offers), which of your accounts they see, and whether they need a passkey. They get a link that works once, for 7 days. A new person chooses their own password; someone who already uses OpenClear confirms with theirs. Nothing is set up until they accept. To invite many, upload a CSV with email, role and, optionally, accounts (account numbers separated by semicolons).
  • Manage a person to change their role, accounts or passkey requirement, or to deactivate them. You can also reset their password, unlock them or sign them out everywhere, unless they also belong to another organization: then only they, or your bank, can.
  • Your organization always keeps at least one administrator.
  • Activity lists every change to your people and sign-on, by your administrators and by your bank.

If your bank approves new people first, someone you invite (or your sign-on or directory adds) shows as waiting for your bank until it decides, and can't sign in yet. You're told what it decides.

Access reviews

Once a quarter, review who has access: Settings → Access reviews → Start a review. Confirm each person, say what you changed, or remove their access. Your bank sees that you've done it.

Sign-on

  • Add connection to connect your identity provider (Entra ID, Okta, Google Workspace, or any OpenID Connect or SAML 2.0 provider), then map its groups to roles. Someone in none of the mapped groups can't sign in.
  • Email domains: add your domain and publish the TXT record shown, at the domain or at _openclear. in front of it, then press Verify. Your connection (and SCIM, if you use it) can then create people with addresses there the first time they sign in. Anyone else needs an invitation.
  • Passwords: once a connection works, use Test sign-in to sign in through it, then turn off password sign-in from that session. Your bank can turn it back on if you're ever locked out.
  • When you sign in through your organization's sign-on, you stay in your organization: to reach another one you belong to, sign out and sign in there.

API clients

  • New API client gives one of your own systems (an accounting or ERP system, say) credentials to call OpenClear's API without a person's password: to send issued checks, or read your exceptions and decisions. Choose its role (one your bank offers), the accounts it can see, and whether it only reads. Its secret is shown once: copy it into your system then.
  • Your system exchanges its client id and secret at POST /api/v1/oauth/token (grant_type=client_credentials) for a token that lasts a short while.
  • Rotate secret if it may have leaked, or Use a key pair so no secret is shared at all. Revoke a client you no longer use: it stops at once.
  • Everything a client does is in your activity under its name. Your bank can see your clients, and revoke them.
