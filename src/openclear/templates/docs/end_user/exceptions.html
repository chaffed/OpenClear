Exceptions & Decisioning

The exceptions queue is where checks and ACH items that couldn't be automatically cleared land for review — a check with no matching issued item, or an ACH transaction not covered by an authorization rule. It's a single, network-agnostic queue: check and ACH exceptions are reviewed the same way.

{{ docs.screenshot("exceptions", "Exceptions queue") }}

The queue opens on Needs attention: items that are still open, or whose recommendation is waiting for approval, oldest first. Change Status to see decided items, or choose All. Each row shows the account (and customer), the amount and date, why the item is an exception (for example "Amount mismatch" or "Stop payment"), its fraud risk, and, where a default disposition applies, when it must be decided by.

Reviewing an exception

{{ docs.screenshot("exception_detail", "Exception detail and decision screen") }}

Each exception puts what was expected next to what was presented, and highlights anything that doesn't match:

  • Checks: the issued check (number, amount, payee, issue date) against the presented one, including the payee read from the check image if there is one. The front and back images appear on the page and open full size. Notes explain stop payments, voided checks, stale dates, and checks already paid.
  • ACH: what the originator is authorized for on this account (amount limit, SEC codes, receiver, frequency) against this transaction, or a note that no authorization is on file.

Fraud risk (High, Medium, or Low) is the model's estimate of how likely the item is to need returning, shown with that estimate as a percentage. It learns from past decisions and is advisory only; items without enough history say "Not scored yet". If a default disposition applies, Decide by shows the deadline in your local time and what happens if nobody decides in time.

How to

  • Decide: choose Pay or Return. Nothing is pre-selected, so an item is never paid by an accidental click. Paying an item with High fraud risk asks you to confirm. Add a reason and, if you like, notes.
  • Recommend a decision: under dual control, one person recommends and a different person approves. The approver sees the recommendation, with its outcome and return reason already selected, and can approve or change it. OpenClear won't let anyone approve their own recommendation. The Approvals menu item shows how many are waiting for you.
  • Return an ACH exception: pick a return reason from your organization's managed list of NACHA codes rather than typing free text.
  • Change a decision: until the item's cut-off, a decision can be changed or taken back. The item says "you can change this until 11:00", with Change decision; right after deciding, the note at the bottom of the screen offers Undo. Under dual control a change is recommended and someone else approves it, and the decision already made stands until they do. At the cut-off the decision becomes final and goes to your bank.

If an exception isn't decided before its deadline, it may be auto-decided by a default disposition your administrator configured. See ML Scoring & Default Disposition.
