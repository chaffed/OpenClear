An API client lets one of your own systems (your accounting or ERP system, say) call OpenClear's API without a person's password: to send issued checks, or read your exceptions and decisions. It signs in with OAuth 2.0 client credentials, its client id and secret, at POST /api/v1/oauth/token.

Each client does only what its role allows, for your accounts (all of them, or the ones you choose). Everything it does is in your activity under its name. Rotate a secret if it may have leaked; revoke a client you no longer use. Your bank can see and revoke your clients too.

An API client lets one of your systems (your core, your online banking provider, your own middleware) call OpenClear's API without a person's password. It signs in with OAuth 2.0 client credentials: its client id and secret, exchanged at POST /api/v1/oauth/token for an access token that lasts a short while.

Each client can do exactly what its security group allows, and only for its customer if you pick one. Everything it does is in the audit log under its name. Rotate a secret if it may have leaked; revoke a client you no longer use (that takes effect at once). Customers you let manage their own API clients make theirs here too, marked "theirs".
